# iOS 16 and 17 verification matrix

[English](two-device-verification-matrix.md) · [简体中文](two-device-verification-matrix.zh-CN.md)

This matrix separates a working transport from a visible App outcome. The two devices are iPhone XR / iOS 16.1.1 / Dopamine rootless and iPhone 14 / iOS 17.3.1 / Relaxin RootHide. Local `cases/` evidence is Git-ignored and must be verified on the testing Mac with `copyapp verify <CASE>`; the committed playbooks are reusable without private device data.

The old XR Translate script tapped `(207,320)` and received an iOS MCP text-dispatch success without visible text. Replaying that exact script in `cases/ios16-translate-old-tap-control-20261017/` failed the same assertion again; its screenshot had a caret but no keyboard. The new shared script taps the visible placeholder at `(100,201)`, shows a keyboard and passes on both phones through both backends. This establishes a focus/tap-dependent difference for this screen, rather than a device-wide iOS MCP text failure.

Before iOS 17 Translate and Clock had completed first use, their launch requests returned errors through iOS MCP and native RSD; an iCTRL Translate launch also returned an error. The screen actually showed SpringBoard's app-specific **Welcome / Accept & Continue** gate, so those errors were not evidence of a missing or broken installed App. The test phone's Translate setup was completed with WLAN Only and optional Siri/Dictation audio sharing declined; Clock's welcome gate was completed. Both Apps then reached their own foreground processes, passed the selected checks above, and passed fresh `survey --launch` cases in `cases/ios17-{translate,clock}-post-onboarding-survey-20261017/` with matching before/after foreground Bundle IDs. Saved failed attempts remain in `cases/ios17-translate-{entry-survey,native-survey,ictrl-launch-probe}-20261017/` and `cases/ios17-clock-{entry-survey,native-survey}-20261017/`. First-use choices are device state; the committed playbooks assume the app can reach its normal UI.

After the v0.1.7 release, the saved XR Calculator and Translate parents and the iOS 17 four-flow Calculator and one-flow Hopper parents passed `copyapp verify` again. The published wheel was downloaded, SHA-256 matched the release record, and its package/version and CLI help loaded from an isolated `/tmp` install. These are artifact and evidence-integrity checks; they do not replay all original/clone flows or prove unrelated App behavior.

Remaining product-level gates are autonomous discovery and new source generation for an unfamiliar App, a standalone Claude CLI run, broader App state and server behavior, and full sandbox interpretation. These are not implied by green doctors, passing selected flows or a release tag. iOS 26 is outside this two-device matrix.
